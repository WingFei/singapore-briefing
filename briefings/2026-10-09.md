## ON THE RADAR

### ASSET MANAGEMENT · SINGAPORE
### Seviora consolidates three managers into one platform

**NEW · 8 October.** Seviora Group said it will integrate Azalea, SeaTown and Seviora Capital, three wholly owned asset-management companies, into a single platform. Reuters reported that the group expects the combination to improve investor access to its investment capabilities. Fullerton Fund Management and InnoVen Capital will remain separate legal entities within the wider group. [Reuters · 8 October, 10:57 PM SGT](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-seviora-integrate-three-asset-management-companies-2026-10-08/)

The transaction is organisational rather than a public takeover, and financial terms were not disclosed. The logic is scale: common distribution, investment infrastructure and client access can reduce duplication across specialised managers. Integration also creates execution questions—how investment mandates, teams, governance and client contracts are harmonised without losing the distinct capabilities that made the boutiques useful.

**WHY IT MATTERS · Analysis:** Singapore is competing as an Asian asset-management centre while institutional allocators demand broader private-market and multi-asset access. A consolidated Seviora platform could present capabilities more coherently to global clients and deepen Temasek-linked financial-sector scale. The test is whether operational simplification produces better client outcomes rather than only a new corporate wrapper.

**WATCH NEXT:** the 2027 implementation timetable, leadership structure, fund-governance arrangements and any disclosed changes to investment teams or client mandates.

Acuity’s opportunity is to use a stable installed base to grow higher-value software; the risk is that portfolio expansion adds complexity without lifting organic growth.

## TECH & AI

### TECH · AI FINANCE
### Revenue disclosure puts AI’s capital stack under the microscope

**NEW · 8 October.** A Financial Times report said OpenAI’s annualised revenue was approximately **US$50 billion** in September—about US$20 billion below a figure previously signalled to investors. Reuters cited the report while explaining Thursday’s semiconductor selloff. The distinction appears to involve reporting methodology, including whether revenue flowing through cloud partners is counted. OpenAI is private; the underlying documents were not independently audited for this briefing. [Financial Times · 8 October](https://www.ft.com/content/b66a9858-f8fb-46cb-b506-44bfe26fca2a) · [Reuters market close · 8 October](https://www.reuters.com/business/wall-st-futures-slide-rising-oil-yields-dampen-mood-2026-10-08/)

The market response matters because AI infrastructure is being financed on expectations of very rapid downstream revenue growth. The semiconductor index fell 3.4%, while Broadcom, Micron and Oracle each dropped more than 4%. One private-company metric does not establish a collapse in demand; it does expose how sensitive public valuations are to definitions, partner economics and the timing of cash generation.

This is a disclosure and interpretation story, not an OpenAI product launch. It also intersects with a broader financing question: data centres, chips and power require capital years before utilisation and margin are certain. Investors will demand clearer separation between gross ecosystem revenue, company-recognised revenue, contracted capacity and cash collected.

**WHY IT MATTERS · Analysis:** Singapore is building data-centre, cloud and enterprise-AI capacity. A higher cost of capital or a reassessment of end-demand could affect project pacing, power procurement and vendor valuations even if model adoption continues. The next proof point is audited or consistently defined operating data, not another annualised run-rate headline.
