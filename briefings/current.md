## ON THE RADAR

### MONETARY POLICY · SINGAPORE
### Markets lean toward a tighter S$ stance on Wednesday

**WATCH · 14 OCTOBER.** All 10 economists polled by Reuters expect the Monetary Authority of Singapore to tighten policy at its scheduled review. The central bank manages the Singapore dollar against a trade-weighted basket rather than setting a headline policy interest rate. A tightening can therefore take the form of a steeper appreciation path, a higher centre or a narrower band; the official decision has not yet been made. [Reuters · 9 October](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapore-poised-tighten-monetary-policy-amid-strong-growth-rising-inflation-2026-10-09/)

The consensus reflects robust activity and renewed inflation risk, including energy costs. Reuters noted that MAS expects 2026 core and headline inflation to average 1.5%–2.5%. A strong consensus is not certainty: policymakers must balance domestic price pressure against a volatile external environment and the lagged effects of past exchange-rate moves.

**WHY IT MATTERS · Analysis:** a firmer S$ path can restrain imported inflation, but it also affects exporters, tourism receipts, translated overseas earnings and the local-currency value of foreign assets. Singapore banks and property vehicles face a second channel through global rates and funding conditions. The decision statement and Macroeconomic Review will matter more than the survey headline.

**WATCH NEXT:** the slope, width and centre of the S$NEER band; MAS’s inflation and growth language; and whether policymakers frame energy and haze-related disruption as temporary or persistent.

## TECH & AI

### TECH · AI FINANCE
### Firmus withdrawal turns AI valuation risk into a financing event

**A$44 billion · proposed valuation withdrawn.** Nvidia- and Blackstone-backed Firmus Technologies abandoned its Australian IPO on 9 October, the day it had planned to publish a prospectus. Reuters Breakingviews said the neocloud and data-centre developer had targeted A$11 a share and a valuation almost three times its August funding-round level. The offering’s withdrawal—not merely a price cut—is an actual financing outcome. [Reuters Breakingviews · 9 October](https://www.reuters.com/commentary/breakingviews/failed-ai-ipo-puts-fear-before-fomo-2026-10-09/)

Firmus’s decision arrives as investors scrutinise how quickly AI infrastructure converts contracted demand and enormous build budgets into cash flow. It does not prove an end to AI demand: the company, its valuation and its project risks are specific. It does show that public-market investors are willing to reject private-market extrapolation when capacity, leverage and customer economics remain uncertain.

The episode follows renewed debate over OpenAI’s annualised revenue definition and weakness in some listed AI-infrastructure names. Together, these are capital-market signals rather than evidence that model usage has reversed. The distinction matters: compute demand can keep growing while the returns available to infrastructure owners deteriorate because of financing costs, power constraints, depreciation and competition.

**WHY IT MATTERS · Analysis:** Singapore is expanding data-centre, cloud and power infrastructure under tight land and energy constraints. A more demanding financing market could slow marginal projects, favour well-capitalised operators and raise the value of credible power and customer contracts. The next useful evidence is audited revenue, utilisation, cash conversion and committed funding—not a headline capacity target.
